# [The Boss Who Never Answered Questions—And Why It Worked](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8aKMxk1kUKk)

A leader who refuses to give answers but demands prepared thinking builds self-reliance, collaboration, and higher standards more effectively than one who simply provides solutions.

## Key Points
- Simon Sinek recounts working under Peter Naggio, a senior leader who never directly answered questions but instead asked "what do you think?" to force independent problem-solving.
- This approach taught self-reliance and critical thinking, but also encouraged collaboration — Sinek had to battle-test ideas with colleagues before presenting them to avoid having holes shot in them.
- The leadership style balanced high standards with support: leaders should push people to bring "thought answers" not just right answers, creating psychological safety through challenge rather than coddling.
- Sinek contrasts this with the mistake of trying to be everyone's friend, noting that friendship-seeking undermines leadership; being friendly is different from being friends.
- The lesson extends to how organizations should develop young talent: expect prepared, battle-tested thinking rather than raw ideas, and provide the push-and-support dynamic that builds genuine confidence.
