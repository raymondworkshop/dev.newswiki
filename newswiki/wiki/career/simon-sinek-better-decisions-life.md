# [Simon Sinek on How to Make Better Choices and Live More Fully](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wij5NZpy6DE)

Simon Sinek argues that better decisions come from using personal filters aligned with purpose and passion rather than financial rewards or convenience, which paradoxically leads to greater professional success and personal fulfillment.

## Key Points
- Decision-making is a process defined by the filters you apply — whether financial rewards, ease of work, or alignment with purpose and passion.
- Sinek's college experience illustrates the shift: choosing classes by low workload led to boredom, while choosing by professor rating brought engagement and excitement to work hard.
- Chasing only money creates a plateau — you eventually burn out or employers lose interest — whereas chasing work that excites you paradoxically yields greater financial rewards because enthusiasm drives performance.
- Sinek's personal filters: "rather be happy than right, rather do good than get rich" — decisions that leave him feeling he contributed, not just compensated.
- The quality of the experience matters: dreading travel to/from engagements signals misaligned filters; excitement about arrival and departure signals alignment.
- Filters are personal choices, not universal judgments — "not right or wrong, just those are my decisions."
