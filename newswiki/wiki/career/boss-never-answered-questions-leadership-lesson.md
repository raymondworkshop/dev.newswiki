# [The Boss Who Never Answered Questions—And Why It Worked](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8aKMxk1kUKk)

Great leadership develops others' judgment by refusing to give answers, instead demanding self-reliant thinking backed by peer collaboration and rigorous standards.

## Key Points
- Simon Sinek recounts his early-career experience with Peter Naggio, a senior leader who refused to answer direct questions and instead asked "what do you think?" to force independent thinking.
- This approach taught self-reliance and the habit of battle-testing ideas with peers before presenting them upward — building both critical thinking and collaboration skills.
- Sinek distinguishes being friendly from being everyone's friend, noting that trying to be universally liked undermines leadership authority.
- Effective leadership combines high standards with supportive challenge: leaders should push people to bring "thought answers" rather than right answers, creating psychological safety through rigorous expectation rather than coddling.
- [AI Synthesis] The method reflects a coaching-oriented leadership model where the leader's primary role is developing others' judgment, not providing solutions.
