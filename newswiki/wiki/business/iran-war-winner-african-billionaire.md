# [Iran War Brings a Surprise Winner: Africa's Richest Man](https://cn.wsj.com/articles/%E4%BC%8A%E6%9C%97%E6%88%98%E4%BA%89%E4%B8%AD%E5%87%BA%E7%8E%B0%E4%BA%86%E4%B8%80%E4%BD%8D%E5%A4%A7%E8%B5%A2%E5%AE%B6-%E9%9D%9E%E6%B4%B2%E9%A6%96%E5%AF%8C-cef2bcd1?mod=cn_hp_biz_pos1)

Dangote's refinery is a strategic response to regional energy instability, turning geopolitical risk into a business opportunity.

## Key Points
- Aliko Dangote's refinery in Nigeria has achieved full operational capacity, producing over 70% more gasoline, diesel, and aviation fuel than in 2025.
- The refinery, originally planned at $20 billion, faced a doubling of costs due to a decade-long delay, but now generates significant returns amid global energy disruptions.
- Dangote's refinery is now the world's largest exporter of aviation fuel, surpassing competitors since April 2026.
- The company plans to expand capacity to 140,000 barrels per day by 2028, with a $130 billion investment, and is building a new refinery in Lamu, Kenya, with a $150 billion budget and a three-year timeline.
- Dangote faces supply challenges due to Nigeria's national oil company (NNPC) not supplying sufficient domestic crude, requiring potential imports to meet demand.

## Business Strategy and Market Positioning
- Dangote leveraged his history of transforming Nigeria from importer to exporter in sectors like sugar and cement to replicate success in refining.
- The refinery addresses Nigeria's long-standing issue of exporting crude oil abroad for refining, which results in lost domestic revenue and high fuel import costs.
- The company plans to build a 1,500-mile pipeline to distribute fuel to inland African nations like Zimbabwe, Botswana, and Zambia, reducing transport costs and expanding market reach.

## Financial and Strategic Impact
- Dangote's net worth increased by $4.86 billion since early 2026, reaching $34.8 billion, according to Bloomberg Billionaires Index.
- The refinery's operations have become a key supply point for Europe and Africa, avoiding reliance on the Strait of Hormuz.
- Dangote plans to list the refinery on the Nigerian Stock Exchange with a [[hubs/valuation|valuation]] of at least $50 billion, followed by a potential secondary listing in New York.
