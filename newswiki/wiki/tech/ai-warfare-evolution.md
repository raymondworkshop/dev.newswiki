# [Warfare Mode is Changing: Is It Gradual Evolution or a Total Revolution?](https://cn.wsj.com/articles/war-tech-change-6a98374a?mod=cn_hp_global_pos1)

The shift to AI-driven, drone-based warfare represents a fundamental change in military operations, not just incremental improvement.

## Key Points
- Drone warfare and low-cost, precise munitions have become irreversible trends, shifting military operations from traditional force projection to decentralized, networked engagements.
- AI-guided autonomous systems are now used in targeting and surveillance—such as in Ukraine’s use of AI-powered drones to detect and pursue fuel convoys.
- The concept of 'air superiority' is being challenged; even nations without air forces can now achieve localized air dominance through drone swarms.
- A new procurement model in Ukraine rewards units based on successful engagements, converting military performance into direct funding for rapid system upgrades—effectively treating weapons as subscription services.
- Military leaders emphasize the need for agility and rapid iteration, noting that the speed of technological change now outpaces traditional military doctrine and training.

## Autonomous Systems and Battlefield Adaptation
- AI is used to analyze battlefield data in real time, enabling faster target acquisition and reducing human error in targeting decisions.
- Ukrainian forces use AI to identify and track enemy vehicles, with drones autonomously pursuing targets after human authorization.
- Russia has deployed AI-guided 'Molniya' drones without human input, due to lack of [[hubs/spacex|Starlink]] connectivity.
- The integration of AI into military operations is still in early stages, but experts predict it will become a dominant factor in the next 5-10 years.

## Implications for Future Conflict
- The traditional military hierarchy and centralized procurement models are being replaced by decentralized, frontline-driven innovation.
- The 'kill chain' is being shortened to under 10 seconds in some cases, allowing for faster response and reduced human intervention.
- A key challenge is the rapid obsolescence of equipment—some drones are rendered obsolete within months due to battlefield adaptation.
- For nations without direct conflict exposure, replicating Ukraine’s model is difficult due to the lack of real-world battlefield feedback and the risk of deploying outdated systems.
