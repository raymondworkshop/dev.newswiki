# [How I use LLMs to learn complex topics](https://laurentiugabriel.github.io/blog/articles/how-i-use-llms-to-learn/)

Building interactive low-poly simulations with LLMs — after verifying a curated knowledge base — creates a more effective, hallucination-free way to master complex technical topics than reading explanations or bulleted lists.

## Key Points
- Standard [[hubs/llm|LLM]] explanations feel too simplistic and emoji-heavy, making them hard to follow for deep technical topics.
- The author uses a three-step flow in plan mode (CC/OpenCode): build foundational knowledge for a topic, verify its accuracy, then generate a low-poly interactive simulation (Rollercoaster Tycoon-style) with responsive controls.
- The resulting simulations — such as [[hubs/chip-manufacturing|ChipTycoon]] tracing sand-to-chip flow, [[hubs/rocket-engine|rocket-engine]], [[hubs/llm-internals|token-town]] for [[hubs/llm|LLM]] internals, [[hubs/f1-engine|engineworks]] for F1 engines, and [[hubs/euv-lithography|euv-lithography]] — are hallucination-free and visually map concepts to objects.
- Retention improves further by adding challenges, quizzes on prior steps, and intuitive puzzles; realism can be enhanced by mapping 3D assets from the author's [[hubs/3d-asset-creation|unreal-game-assets-creation-skill]].
- [AI Synthesis] This workflow turns passive reading into active, visual, systems-level learning that scales across semiconductor, aerospace, and AI infrastructure domains.
