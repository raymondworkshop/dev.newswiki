# [How to Improve Yourself Right NOW (and Why) - Prof. Jordan Peterson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PE0u7-SX2hs)

Jordan Peterson argues that meaningful self-improvement begins by identifying and fixing small, immediate problems in your daily environment and routines — since they constitute most of your life — and by consciously aiming at the right goals, which reshapes how you perceive and interact with the world.

## Key Points
- Self-improvement is not casual self-help — it reduces unnecessary suffering for yourself and those around you, since disorganization exacts a real cost. [[lifestyle/_index|Lifestyle]]
- Start by looking around your immediate environment (e.g., your room) for things that bother you and that you can actually fix — papers, clutter, tangled cables — and fix them. [[hubs/jordan-peterson|Jordan Peterson]]
- Daily routines (brushing teeth, breakfast, commute) constitute roughly 50% of your life; optimizing these mundane repetitions yields outsized returns. [[career/_index|Career]]
- Fix a hundred small things in your broader domain of being and you eliminate many traps and sources of 'stupid pain.'
- Stay within your domain of competence: don't try to fix problems you lack the skill for (e.g., a homeless schizophrenic alcoholic); find what you *can* repair. [[hubs/jordan-peterson|Jordan Peterson]]
- When you set a genuine aim, your mind reconfigures perception to reveal relevant details — 'you see what you aim at' (inattentional blindness / gorilla experiment). [[lifestyle/_index|Lifestyle]]
- If the world manifests negatively, ask whether you are aiming at the right thing — but bind this with humility: random misfortune (illness, accidents) also exists.
