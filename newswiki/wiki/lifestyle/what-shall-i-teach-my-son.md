# [What Shall I Teach My Son?](https://stevekrouse.com/ends)

In the age of AGI, the most honest approach to education is to collaboratively craft its purpose with our children rather than impose predetermined answers.

## Key Points
- The author, expecting his first child, questions what to teach his son when programming — his economically valuable skill — feels threatened by AGI. [[hubs/agi|AGI]]
- Neil Postman's 'The End of Education' (1995) shows schools have survived radio, film, TV, and the internet by shifting purposes (clergy training → citizenship → job preparation), suggesting institutional resilience via the [[hubs/lindy-effect|Lindy Effect]].
- Past techno-optimism (Edison on film, the author on MOOCs in 2013) repeatedly overestimated technology's power to replace schools, which persist due to prosaic forces: endowments, prestige, childcare. [[hubs/edison|Thomas Edison]]
- The market once served as an objective arbiter of 'good' (Jobs, Gates, Einstein), but AI now threatens both entrepreneurial and academic paths to distinction, undermining 'earn a living' as education's stabilizing purpose. [[career/_index|Career]]
- Postman proposes new positive purposes: 'Spaceship Earth' (planetary stewardship), 'The American Experiment' (civic wonder), and 'The World Makers' (students as tool/narrative creators). [[hubs/neil-postman|Neil Postman]]
- The medium is the message (Postman/McLuhan): how we teach matters more than what; Alfie Kohn's 'doing with' vs 'doing to' implies relating to children as humans, not objects. [[hubs/alfie-kohn|Alfie Kohn]]
- [AI Synthesis] The author concludes that inviting children to co-author the purpose of their education is the most honest response to radical uncertainty.
